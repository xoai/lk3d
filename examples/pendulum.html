A pendulum's period

Drag a ball aside and let go. The page solves the swing itself, θ″ = −(g/ℓ)·sin θ, and moves the bobs (set({ move })); the strings, the arcs and the forces are lines, and the angle at pivot A is one of the scene's dimensions, measured from the bob as it is drawn. The textbook's period, 2π√(ℓ/g), is right only for small swings: release them at 50° to see by how much.

A period is measured from one upward crossing of the vertical to the next. The exact period of a swing of amplitude α₀ is 2π√(ℓ/g) / M(1, cos ½α₀), M the arithmetic–geometric mean.

Period against length

Solid: 2π√(ℓ/g). Dashed: the exact period for A's swing. The dot is bob A as measured: on the solid line for a small swing, above it for a wide one.

The model: no air drag, a string with no mass that does not stretch, g = 9.81 m/s²; θ″ = −(g/ℓ)·sin θ solved by Runge–Kutta (RK4) in steps of 2 ms. The force arrows are to scale, 1.5 cm for each newton. The stand and the balls are drawn at their true size.
