Two-source interference

A ripple tank: two dippers beat on the water and their circular waves cross. The water is a surface the page shapes each frame (fig.surface, from the live add-on): a height and a tone for each of 161 × 113 points, each the sum of the two waves there. The dippers slide along the bar (set({ move })), the lines of still water and the paths to P are lines, and P is a plane handle: drag it across the water, reach it with Tab (two sliders, one for each direction), or click the water to put it there (onPress and fig.hit).

The ripples are drawn higher than they are, and slowed down: in a real tank the dippers beat about ten times a second.

Why

Each source sends out y = A·e−βr/√(1 + r/λ)·sin(ωt − 2πr/λ + φ): smaller as it spreads round a longer circle and as the water damps it. At P the two add. When S₂ beats with S₁ (φ = 0), they arrive in step where Δd = kλ, the water there swinging most, and in opposition where Δd = (k + ½)λ, the water there still. A delay φ moves every line: still water where Δd = (k + ½ + φ/360°)λ.

Each line of still water is a hyperbola with S₁ and S₂ as its foci: the points whose distances to them differ by one amount.
